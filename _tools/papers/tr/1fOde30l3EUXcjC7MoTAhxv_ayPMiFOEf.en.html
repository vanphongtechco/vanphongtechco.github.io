Dependence of the Lifelength of a Hydraulic Breaker Pick on the Wear Resistance of Its Material

V.I. Bolobov, Le Thanh Binh, S.A. Chupin, V.A. Plashchinsky

MIAB. Mining Informational and Analytical Bulletin (Gornyy informatsionno-analiticheskiy byulleten’), 2020, No. 5, pp. 68–79 · DOI: 10.25018/0236-1493-2020-5-0-68-79 · UDC 620.172.2

Abstract

The paper presents experimental results on the effect of blow energy on the percussion–abrasion wear intensity of a model tool in the form of a conical pick, made of three grades of tool steel, during percussive interaction with a granite block. Part of the tools was subjected to the standard heat treatment practised at pick manufacturing plants, while the other part received additional cold treatment or cryogenic treatment. To justify the transfer of the experimental data to full-scale conditions, the operating parameters of the experiment were set so that its conditions were similar to the working conditions of a hydraulic breaker pick. To achieve this similarity, the level of contact stresses between the model tool and the rock in the experiments was the same as in percussive penetration of the pick. A relationship between the percussion–abrasion wear intensity and the blow energy of the model tool was established, and on this basis the wear intensities of the pick of a JCB HM380 hydraulic breaker during the breaking of granite blocks were calculated. The pick lifelength was evaluated, taken as the number of blows delivered by the pick until complete wear of its tip. From a comparison of the lifelength of picks made of the steels studied and subjected to the above types of heat treatment, it is concluded that it is expedient to introduce cold treatment and cryogenic treatment operations into the manufacturing process of hydraulic breaker picks.

Keywords: hydraulic breaker pick, percussive action, blow energy, granite, percussion–abrasion wear, contact stress, tip dulling, cold treatment, cryogenic treatment, pick lifelength.

Introduction

A considerable number of works, for example [1–14], are devoted to the study of the interaction of a percussive tool with the rock being broken and to methods of increasing its wear resistance.

In the authors' work [15], experimental results were presented on the wear of the blunt (dulled) area of a cone-shaped model tool as a result of its repeated percussive penetration into a granite block. It was found that the obtained dependences of the tool mass loss on the number of blows are described by an inverse power function of the form Δm = An–B, where A and B are coefficients depending on the material and type of heat treatment of the tool, with 3 stages, each differing in the amount of metal mass loss and the rate of its decrease with increasing number of blows. Using the presented mathematical model, it was shown that the transition from one wear stage to another is determined by the ratio between the stresses reached at the contact between the tool and the rock and the ultimate strength and yield strength of the tool material exhibited under dynamic conditions. At the final stage of wear, which is the longest, the metal mass loss per blow does not change significantly with the number of blows and can serve as an indicator of the percussion–abrasion wear intensity of a tool made of the given steel, Kм [mg per blow].

In the present paper, the dependence of Kм [15] on the blow energy Aм of the model tool is established and used to estimate the percussion–abrasion wear intensity Kп of a hydraulic breaker pick and its lifelength (number of blows) until complete wear of the tip.

Experimental Procedure

The general view and the diagram of the experimental setup are shown in Fig. 1.

The model tool 2 (see Fig. 1), in the form of a conical pick with a tip angle β = 30° and a cylindrical-part radius rм = 4 mm (required to satisfy the similarity criterion), was weighed on an analytical balance (±0.1 mg) and then fixed in the massive striker 3 of the setup.

[Figure 2 — see original PDF, page 6] Surface of the granite block after a series of blows by the model tool

A cylindrical granite core sample 1 from the Vyborg massif, with a hardness of f = 13 on the M.M. Protodyakonov scale and a uniaxial compressive strength σсж ~200 MPa, was placed under the tool. Tool 2 together with striker 3 fell freely from a given height (hм = 42 mm), striking the surface of the sample with its tip. The pre-impact velocity of the tool, calculated from h, was 0.91 m/s. With the same model tool, a series of n blows was delivered to the surface of sample 1, each subsequent blow being displaced by a distance ≥ 3dл, where dл is the diameter of the crater (Fig. 2).

After each blow, the mass of the model tool was measured to determine its loss Δm due to the blow. With a large number of blows (over 40), Δm per blow was determined over an interval of blows (n = 10–100) as the quotient of the total value of the function over n blows divided by the number n in the interval. By varying the mass of striker 3 (2.8; 4.2; 5.6 and 7.0 kg), the effect of the kinetic energy of the blow (Aм = 1.16; 1.74; 2.32 and 2.90 J) on the form of the dependence of tool mass loss Δm on the number of blows n was investigated.

Steels 38ХМ, У8А and Х12МФ were chosen as model tool materials. The first is a typical material of picks of domestic (Russian) and foreign (AISI P20 steel) manufacture. The second is the material of percussive teeth of active-action excavator buckets, which performed satisfactorily in tests [16], and the third is a material with high hardness and toughness in the hardened state, which has undergone field tests [16] as a hardfacing to prevent tooth wear.

Part of the tools made of these steels was subjected to the standard heat treatment (ТТО) practised at pick manufacturing plants — oil quenching at 800–1000 °C followed by tempering at 200 °C; the other part received additional cold treatment (holding after quenching at minus 75 °C for 5 h) or cryogenic treatment (holding after quenching for 20 h in liquid nitrogen at minus 196 °C), as operations widely used to increase the wear resistance of metal-cutting tools [17–26].

Justification of the Similarity of the Penetration Processes of the Model Tool and the Hydraulic Breaker Pick into Rock

To justify the transfer of the experimental data obtained in the laboratory with the model tool to full-scale conditions, the operating parameters of the experiment were set so that its conditions were similar to the working conditions of the hydraulic breaker pick.

Based on the conclusion of K.I. Ivanov [27], it was assumed that, to ensure similarity of the conditions of penetration of the model percussive tool and the pick into rock, the experiment must provide a level of contact stresses between the model tool and the rock close to that occurring during percussive penetration of the pick. This guarantees the same cyclic durability of the tool and unchanged energy consumption for breaking a unit volume of rock.

The physical equation of the process of penetration of the tool and the pick into rock was represented as the functional

φ = (A, EME, μME, r, β, σk, EП, μП),     (1)

where A, EME, μME, r, β, σk, EП, μП are the blow energy, the modulus of elasticity and Poisson's ratio of the tool, the radius of the cylindrical part and the tip angle of the model tool and the pick; the contact stress; and the modulus of elasticity and Poisson's ratio of the rock.

Dimensions of the parameters determining the penetration process:

[β] = [L0M0T0] ⇒ P5,
[σk] = [L–1M1T–2] ⇒ P6,
[EП] = [L–1M1T–2] ⇒ P7,
[μП] = [L0M0T0] ⇒ P8,
[A] = [L2M1T–2] ⇒ P1,
[EME] = [L–1M1T–2] ⇒ P2,
[μME] = [L0M0T0] ⇒ P3,
[r] = [L1M0T0] ⇒ P4,     (2)

where L is length; M is mass; T is time.

Thus, the complete dimensional matrix had a size of n×q, where n = 8 is the total number of parameters determining the penetration process and q = 3 is the number of basic units of measurement of the parameters.

After excluding from (2) the dimensionless parameters (P3, P5 and P8) and reducing parameters with identical dimensions to one (in our case, of the parameters P2, P6 and P7 it is expedient to keep P6), a reduced matrix of the following form was obtained

‖A‖ = ‖ 2   1   –2 ‖ ⇒ A
        ‖ –1   1   –2 ‖ ⇒ σk
        ‖ 1   0   0 ‖ ⇒ r     (3)

The total number of similarity criteria Nπ, determined as Nπ = m – l (where m and l are the total number of parameters (A, σk and r) and the number of independent parameters (A and r)), is 3 – 2 = 1 in our case. The specific expression of the similarity criterion π for the independent parameters A and r had the form

π = P3(P1x, P2y)–1 = σk(Ax, ry)–1,     (4)

where x and y are unknown exponents of the independent parameters to be determined to reveal the specific form of the similarity criterion.

Since the similarity criterion is a dimensionless quantity, the dimensions of the numerator and the denominator must be the same, and from relation (4) we wrote

[σk] = [A]x[r]y.     (5)

Expanding the dimensions of the parameters of the last expression

[L–1M1T–2] = [L2M1T–2]x[L1M0T0]y     (6)

and solving the resulting system of equations

2x + y = –1
x = 1
–2x = –2     (7)

we obtained x = 1; y = –3.

Thus, the similarity criterion for the modelling conditions (πм) and the pick working conditions (πп) had the form

π = idem ⇔ πМ = πП ⇔ σk,МAМ–1rМ3 = σk,ПAП–1rП3     (8)

Hence, to ensure equality of σк, we obtained

σk,М / σk,Н = (AМ / AП)·(rМ / rП)–3,     (9)

where Aм and rм; Aп and rп are the blow energies and cylindrical-part radii of the model tool and of the hydraulic breaker pick, respectively.

Having chosen the drop height of the model tool hм = 42 mm as convenient for the experiment, we obtained a blow energy Aм = 1.16 J (for a striker mass of 2.8 kg). After substituting Aм into (9) together with the values Aп, rп of the hydraulic breaker pick (Aп = 981 J, rп = 39 mm for the JCB HM380 hydraulic breaker), the cylindrical-part radius of the model tool rм = 4 mm, at which the similarity condition is satisfied, was established.

Thus, to model the conditions of pick penetration into rock, the drop height hм of the tool and its radius rм should be 42 mm and 4 mm, respectively, which was implemented in the experiments.

As for ensuring equality of the energy consumption for rock breaking, a comparison of this indicator for the experimental conditions (which, based on the data of [29], can be estimated at 14 MJ/m3) with the corresponding indicator for the hydraulic breaker (10.4 MJ/m3 [16]) showed that their values are close.

Consequently, when the established values of hм and rм are ensured in the experiment, force and energy similarity of the systems "model percussive tool" – "rock" and "pick" – "rock" is maintained.

Experimental Results

The results of experiments on the effect of blow energy Aм on the mass loss of the model tool and its percussion–abrasion wear intensity Kм are illustrated in Fig. 3 using the example of a tool made of 38ХМ steel.

It turned out that, as the blow energy Aм increases, the curves ΣΔm = f(n) for the model tool made of all steels shift upward (Fig. 3), and the percussion–abrasion wear intensity Kм increases. The values of Kм and Aм are in direct proportion: when Aм increases n times, Kм increases practically by the same factor. For example, when the blow work increases from Aм1 = 1.16 J to Aм4 = 2.9 J, i.e. by a factor of 2.5, the ratio of wear intensities Kм4/Kм1 is 2.41 for 38ХМ steel, 2.63 for У8А and 2.37 for Х12МФ (Fig. 4), i.e. the following equality is practically observed

KMi = KM1 · AMi / AM1     (10)

Since the similarity criterion (9) was satisfied in the experiments, it was assumed that the established relationship (10) between Kм and Aм can be used to determine the percussion–abrasion wear intensity of the hydraulic breaker pick Kп from the wear intensity Kм of a model tool made of the corresponding material and the ratio of the blow energies of the pick Aп and the tool Aм

KП = KM · AП / AM     (11)

Determination of the Percussion–Abrasion Wear Intensity of the Hydraulic Breaker Pick and Its Lifelength

[Figure 5 — see original PDF, page 10] Adopted scheme of the change in the dulling radius of the pick as a result of wear

It was assumed that the limit state of the pick (end of its lifelength) occurs upon complete wear of its tip, i.e. when the radius rсф of the hemispherical dulling of the pick tip becomes equal to the radius of its cylindrical part

rсфк = rп     (12)

Assuming that the overwhelming part of the worn metal comes from the contact area of the pick with the rock, the number of blows N* that the pick must deliver to the granite block for the radius rсф to change from rсф0 = 13 mm (dulling radius of the initial pick) to rсфк = rп = 39 mm was calculated, owing to the reduction of the hemisphere volume at each blow by the amount v' corresponding to Kп,

v' = KП / ρ     (13)

where ρ is the density of the pick material (7.85·103 kg/m3 for all steels) (Fig. 5). The established value N* was taken as the pick lifelength.

The values of Kм of the model tool established in the experiments (for Aм1 = 1.16 J), the wear intensities Kп calculated from them by (18) (for Aп = 981 J), the volumes v' corresponding to Kп, and the established values of N* are presented in the table.

As can be seen from a comparison of the data in the table, if the traditionally used pick material, 38ХМ steel, is replaced with more wear-resistant materials — У8А and Х12МФ steels — the tool lifelength will increase from 4.3·105 to 6.1·105 and 8.6·105 blows, respectively, i.e. by factors of 1.4 and 2.

Cold treatment and cryogenic treatment of tools made of all the steels considered can increase by a factor of 1.1 to 2 the number of blows that the pick can deliver before complete dulling, compared with a similar pick after standard heat treatment.

This positive effect of cold treatment is explained by the fact that holding tools made of the high-carbon steels У8А (~0.8% C) and Х12МФ (~1.5% C) at sub-zero temperatures leads to a significant reduction in the content of "soft" retained austenite in their structure, where it remained in considerable amounts after quenching. It also promotes an increase in the carbide content in the structure of tools made of the alloy steels 38ХМ and Х12МФ. Moreover, the lower the cold treatment temperature, the higher its effectiveness [29].

Main Conclusions

  • A laboratory test procedure is presented for studying the wear patterns of a model percussive tool made of various steels with various heat treatments under conditions simulating the wear conditions of a hydraulic breaker pick during its penetration into rock.
  • Tests of the model tool made of three grades of tool steel showed that its percussion–abrasion wear intensity Kм during penetration into a granite block increases with blow energy in direct proportion.
  • Based on the established wear intensities Kм of the model tool and the form of their dependence on blow energy, the percussion–abrasion wear intensity Kп and the lifelength of a JCB HM380 hydraulic breaker pick made of three steel grades and subjected to three types of heat treatment were calculated for penetration into granite.
  • It is shown that if the traditionally used pick material, 38ХМ steel, is replaced with more wear-resistant materials — У8А and Х12МФ steels — the number of blows the pick can deliver before complete wear of its tip (pick lifelength) will increase by factors of 1.4 and 2. Cold treatment and cryogenic treatment of tools made of these steels can increase the pick lifelength by a factor of 1.1 to 2 compared with standard heat treatment.
