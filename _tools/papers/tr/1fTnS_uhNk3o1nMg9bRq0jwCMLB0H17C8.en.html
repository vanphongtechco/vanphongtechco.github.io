Increasing the wear resistance of hydraulic breaker pick material by cold treatment

Le Thanh Binh, V.I. Bolobov, G.A. Yusupov, Tran Dinh Bao

4th International Scientific and Practical Conference "Innovations in Transport and Mechanical Engineering", Saint Petersburg Mining University, April 28–29, 2016, pp. 164–168 · UDC 620.172.2

Abstract

It is shown that cold treatment of hydraulic breaker pick materials – steels 38ХМ, 55С2, У8 and Х12МФА (5-hour holding of specimens at −75 °C) – reduces the retained austenite content (by 44 ÷ 88%) and increases the carbide content (by 15 ÷ 98%) in hardened steel, which is accompanied by a slight increase in the strength characteristics (σ0.2, σB) and hardness of the tested materials, with a simultaneous decrease in impact toughness. As a result of exposure to cold, the abrasive wear resistance increases substantially (by 18 ÷ 35%).

Keywords: hydraulic breaker picks, carbon and alloy steels, cold treatment, increase in wear resistance.

When excavators with an active-action bucket are operated on the hard and abrasive rocks typical of Vietnam's non-metallic raw-material deposits, the main cause of failure of the working tool – the picks – is their limiting wear (Fig. 1), caused by the insufficient wear resistance of the materials used – high-carbon and alloy steels.

It is known [1, 2, 3] that if the martensite finish point Mk of a steel lies below room temperature, then after quenching retained austenite remains in the structure of the material, and its amount increases with increasing content of carbon and alloying elements in the steel. The presence of retained austenite in the structure of the material of hardened parts adversely affects its physical and mechanical properties and manifests itself, first of all, in reduced hardness and, as a consequence, reduced wear resistance.

[Figure 1 — see original PDF, page 2] Hydraulic breaker pick (ATLAS COPCO HB2500) made of steel 40ХН with a worn head part (left) after 58 days of work breaking oversized granite blocks at a quarry in the Binh Dinh area (Vietnam)

Cold treatment, which consists in cooling hardened steel below its Mk temperature, promotes the transformation of retained austenite into martensite and the formation, during subsequent tempering, of a large number of very fine carbide particles, which also contributes to improving the service properties of the steel [8, 13]. For this reason, cold treatment is widely used as an additional operation after quenching to improve the wear resistance of tool steels [1, 4-7, 9-12, 14]. At the same time, no information on its use for improving the service properties of mining tool materials subjected to abrasive wear has been found in the literature. The present work compares the structure, mechanical properties (σ0.2, σB, KCV, HRC) and abrasive wear resistance (Rw) of several steels most widely used as pick materials (38ХМ, 55С2, У8 and Х12МФА) after the heat treatment usually applied in practice (oil quenching plus low or medium tempering) and after additional cold treatment. In the latter case, the specimens were placed, immediately after quenching, in a cryostat with a cooling liquid at a temperature of −75 °C, where they were held for 5 hours.

Microstructural studies and determination of the carbide and retained austenite contents in the steel were carried out using an Axioplan-2 microscope. Physical and mechanical characteristics were determined on standard five-fold specimens using a Zwick/Roell Z100 tensile testing machine, a Zwick/Roell RKP450 pendulum impact tester and a Zwick/Roell ZHU hardness tester. Wear resistance tests were carried out according to the ASTM G99 method [5] (the “pin-on-disk” method) on cylindrical pin specimens (4 mm in diameter, 14 mm in height), which were worn under a constant load (P = 40 N) at a speed of 0.257 m/s against an electrocorundum disk. From the results of weighing the specimens before and after exposure to the abrasive, the wear resistance was calculated by the formula:

WR = ρ·P·L / m, N·m/mm3,    (1)

where ρ is the density of the pin material, g/mm3; m is the mass loss of the pin due to wear, g; L is the wear path (100 m).

As follows from Figs. 2 and 3, as well as Tables 1 and 2, cold treatment, as in [1, 2, 3], leads to a significant decrease in the retained austenite content (by 44 ÷ 88%) and an increase in the carbide content (by 15 ÷ 98%) in hardened steel. This change in structure is accompanied by a slight increase in the strength characteristics (σ0.2, σB) and hardness of the tested materials, with a simultaneous decrease in impact toughness. (The σ0.2 and σB values of steels 55С2 and Х12МФА could not be determined because of the large scatter in the experimental results, caused, as can be concluded, by embrittlement of the specimen material as a result of heat treatment.) Moreover, as the austenitizing temperature for quenching and the tempering temperature decrease, this effect of cold treatment increases. As a result of exposure to cold, the abrasive wear resistance increases by 18 ÷ 35%.

From the research results presented, it can be concluded that cold treatment can be regarded as a promising additional heat-treatment operation for increasing the wear resistance of steels used to manufacture picks of hydraulic breakers working on hard, highly abrasive rocks. In doing so, the most optimal heat-treatment modes should be found at which the negative effect of cold treatment on the impact toughness of the steel will be minimal.
