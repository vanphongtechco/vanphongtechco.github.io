Cold Treatment as a Way to Improve the Wear Resistance of Drilling Tool Material

Le Thanh Binh, V. I. Bolobov, G. A. Yusupov, M. P. Moseev

Gornyi Informatsionno-Analiticheskiy Byulleten (Mining Informational and Analytical Bulletin, GIAB), 2016, No. 11, pp. 108–114 · ISSN 0236-1493 · UDC 622.23.05

Abstract

The paper presents the results of experiments studying the effect of cold treatment (holding samples at -75 °C) on the basic mechanical properties (σ0.2, σB, KCV, HRC) and wear resistance of the drill steels 38ХМ and 55С2 after quenching from different temperatures. A significant increase in wear resistance (by 18–26%, respectively) was found, with a slight increase in strength (by 1–1.1%) and a decrease in impact toughness (by 1.4–19%) of the samples subjected to cold treatment, compared with materials that underwent conventional heat treatment. It is concluded that cold treatment can be regarded as a promising additional heat-treatment operation for increasing the wear resistance of steels used to manufacture mining working tools – hydraulic breaker picks, drill rods, bit bodies and other mining equipment operating under abrasive wear conditions.

Keywords: drill steels, cold treatment, heat treatment, wear resistance.

It is known [1, 2, 3] that if the martensite finish point Mf of a steel lies below room temperature, retained austenite remains in the structure of the material after quenching; its amount increases with the content of carbon and alloying elements in the steel (Fig. 1). The higher their content, the lower the Mf point (Fig. 2).

The presence of retained austenite in the structure of the material of quenched parts adversely affects its physical and mechanical properties, manifesting itself above all in reduced hardness and strength and in deteriorated thermal conductivity and magnetic characteristics. These material properties are of primary importance for mechanical engineering technology. The thermal conductivity of steel has a significant effect on the rate of heat removal from the edge of a cutting tool. It has been established experimentally that the reduced durability of cutters during high-speed cutting on metal-working machine tools is explained by the high temperature arising at the edge, which is caused by the poor thermal conductivity of austenite [1].

[Figure 1 — see original PDF, page 2] Dependence of the amount of retained austenite on the content of alloying elements [2].

Cold treatment, which consists in cooling quenched steel below its Mf temperature, promotes the transformation of retained austenite into martensite, which improves the wear resistance and mechanical properties of the material. In addition, prolonged holding at a temperature well below Mf leads, during subsequent tempering of the steel, to the formation of a large number of very fine carbide particles, called η-carbide, which also contributes to improving the service properties of the steel [4]. The effect of cold treatment depends on the amount of retained austenite in the quenched steel and increases as the Mf point decreases. Therefore, cold treatment is most effective for carbon steels with a carbon content above 0.6% and for high-alloy steels.

Cold treatment and cryogenic treatment are widely used in engineering as an additional operation after quenching to improve the wear resistance of tool steels [1]. Thus, according to [5] (Table 1), holding quenched samples of a wide range of alloy and high-speed steels used as materials for cutting tools and for cold and hot forming dies leads to a substantial improvement (up to 2.5 and 7 times, respectively) in their wear resistance (the test procedure is not given in [5]).

At the same time, no information on the use of these types of treatment to improve the service properties of mining tool materials subject to abrasive wear has been found in the literature. The present work compares the abrasive wear resistance and mechanical properties of some of the most widely used drill steels after cold treatment with the characteristics of these materials after the heat treatment commonly applied in practice, and discusses the possibility of using the cold treatment operation in mining machine building.

The objects of study were the basic physical and mechanical characteristics (σ0.2, σB, KCV, HRC) and the resistance to abrasive wear (Rw) of steels 38ХМ and 55С2, as materials for picks of percussive drilling equipment, determined on samples after the heat treatment typical for such steels (oil quenching plus low or medium tempering) and on samples additionally subjected to cold treatment. In the latter case, the samples were placed immediately after quenching in a cryostat with a coolant at -75 °C, where they were held for 4 h.

Experiments to determine the physical and mechanical characteristics were carried out on standard specimens using a Zwick/Roell Z100 tensile testing machine, a Zwick/Roell RKP450 pendulum impact tester and a Zwick/Roell ZHU hardness tester. Wear resistance tests were performed according to ASTM G99 (pin-on-disk method) on cylindrical pin specimens (4 mm in diameter, 14 mm high), which under a constant load (P = 40 N) were worn for a set time at a speed of 0.257 m/s against an electrocorundum disk. From the results of weighing the specimens before and after exposure to the abrasive, the wear resistance was calculated by the formula:

WR = ρ · P · L / m, N·m/mm3   (1)

where ρ is the density of the pin material, g/mm3; m is the mass loss of the pin as a result of wear, g; L is the wear path (100 m).

As follows from the data in the table, cold treatment, compared with conventional heat treatment, as in [1, 2, 3], leads to a slight increase in the strength characteristics (σ0.2, σB) and hardness of the tested materials with a simultaneous decrease in impact toughness. (The values of σ0.2 and σB of steel 55С2 could not be determined because of the large scatter in the experimental results, caused, as may be concluded, by embrittlement of the specimens as a result of heat treatment.) Moreover, with increasing austenitizing and tempering temperatures, this effect of cold treatment decreases. Abrasive wear resistance increases by 18 to 26% as a result of exposure to cold.

From the preliminary research results presented, it can be concluded that cold treatment can be regarded as a promising additional heat-treatment operation for increasing the wear resistance of steels used to manufacture mining working tools – hydraulic breaker picks, drill rods, bit bodies and other mining equipment operating under abrasive wear conditions. In doing so, the optimal heat-treatment modes should be found under which the negative effect of cold treatment on the impact toughness of steel will be minimal.
